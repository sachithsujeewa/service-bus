# Documentation Rediscovery Charter

## Mission

Rediscover the logical documentation distributed across the historical event and webhook pages, then restructure it into a professional chain from business purpose through requirements, architecture, design, technology, implementation, and guidance while preserving the original meaning.

## Problem statement

The source material mixes goals, concepts, user instructions, requirements, architecture, detailed design, tables, configuration, implementation procedures, experiments, unresolved questions, and proposed improvements. The information exists, but its reasoning chain is difficult to follow. Rewriting page by page would preserve that fragmentation.

## In scope

- Inventorying all exported pages and attachments.
- Extracting why, what, and how statements.
- Classifying content by documentation layer.
- Consolidating duplicates and improving professional wording.
- Mapping business needs to requirements, architecture, design, technology, implementation, and guidance.
- Recording source provenance, ambiguity, contradictions, questions, redactions, and missing information.
- Measuring documentation maturity and traceability completeness.

## Out of scope

- Inventing requirements not present in the source.
- Recommending or selecting replacement technology.
- Redesigning or implementing the system.
- Silently updating historical claims to current state.
- Resolving unanswered source questions by editorial assumption.

Current-system validation or future-state design may be commissioned later, but it must remain separate from faithful documentation restructuring.

## Principles

1. **Meaning before wording:** understand the source before editing it.
2. **Preserve intent:** never change the strength or meaning of an original statement.
3. **Why before what before how:** business context precedes requirements; requirements precede architecture and design; technology and implementation follow.
4. **Trace every statement:** derived content links backward to source and forward to related layers.
5. **Show gaps honestly:** silence in the source remains a documented gap.
6. **Separate content classes:** original material, editorial clarification, validation evidence, and future proposals must be distinguishable.
7. **Deduplicate without losing provenance:** consolidated text retains every applicable source link.
8. **One navigable structure:** readers can move from purpose to implementation without knowing the original page history.

## Success measures

- 100% of 27 exported source pages have a documented disposition.
- 100% of local attachments are retained and linked where relevant.
- Every target section identifies its source pages.
- Every extracted requirement is supported by source wording.
- No suggestion or unresolved question is presented as an approved requirement.
- Business, requirements, architecture, design, technology, and implementation layers have explicit traceability.
- Repetition is consolidated without loss of unique details.
- Missing, contradictory, sensitive, or obsolete-looking content is flagged rather than silently repaired.

## Definition of done

- Complete source content register.
- Approved hierarchy from [[00-Target-Documentation-Structure]].
- Faithfully rewritten documents for every layer.
- Source-to-target and layer-to-layer traceability matrix.
- Documentation maturity assessment.
- Question, contradiction, missing-information, and editorial-change registers.
- Review evidence showing that no source content or attachment was accidentally omitted.
